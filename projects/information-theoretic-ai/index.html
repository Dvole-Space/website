Projects / Information-Theoretic AI

Information-Theoretic Theory of Artificial Intelligence, Recording, and Invention

Active

As humans generate and record more knowledge about the universe, we repeatedly invent new ways to keep that growing informational complexity manageable—language, writing, mathematics, science, computers, and now AI. The initial hypothesis was that these inventions may be understood as transformations that extract, organize, compress, or preserve useful structure from an ever-expanding body of recorded information. The exact mathematical object is still open: it may involve entropy, predictive information, rate-distortion, decision-relevant information, computation, or something else entirely.

The broader goal is to turn that intuition into a rigorous theory of intelligence and representation, and eventually ask whether it can give us a deeper way to characterize AI capability than collections of empirical benchmarks. Rather than committing early to a particular metric or framework, the project is deliberately exploratory: review the relevant literature, kill weak formulations, develop better ones, and preserve the core question throughout—whether AI can be understood in terms of how effectively a system makes the structure of an expanding informational universe usable.
